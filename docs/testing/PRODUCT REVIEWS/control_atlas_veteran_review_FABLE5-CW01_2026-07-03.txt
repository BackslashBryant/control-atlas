1. ADOPTION VERDICT (BLUF)
----------------------------------------------------------------
- SHELFWARE (with a visible path to SITUATIONAL if data fidelity
  is fixed).
- The single biggest reason: the control text it labels "OFFICIAL
  TEXT / SOURCE EXCERPT" is not the official text — every
  organization-defined parameter has been silently stripped,
  leaving broken sentences — and the flagship worksheet export
  includes withdrawn controls (AC-13, SA-12, SA-13) as things to
  implement. Once I catch a tool lying to me about control text,
  I have to verify everything it says, and at that point it costs
  me more time than it saves.

2. NINETY-SECOND GUT CHECK
----------------------------------------------------------------
- Does the builder understand the job? PARTLY.
- What tipped me off, good: the vocabulary is right in a way
  fakers never get right. Reciprocity failure patterns, control
  inheritance vs customer-side responsibility, ATO vs ATC,
  authorization boundary scope creep, "do not inherit from a
  provider without an active ATO" — those phrases come from
  someone who has at least sat near this work. No login, no
  onboarding wizard blocking the door, no data upload. Honest
  "what this is not" page that admits it is not a GRC tool.
- What tipped me off, bad: ninety seconds in, I opened AC-2 —
  the first control anyone opens — and read "Require for group
  and role membership" and "Require approvals by for requests to
  create accounts." That is what 800-53 looks like when you strip
  the ODP assignments and keep walking. Anyone who has written an
  SSP control statement trips over that hole instantly. The
  builder either did not notice or did not think it mattered.
  Both are bad.
----------------------------------------------------------------
3. SCOPE CALL
----------------------------------------------------------------
- What it actually is, in its own terms: a public, browser-only,
  no-account reference cross-walk (controls, CCIs, baselines,
  ZT activities, a couple of program frameworks) plus a blank
  template generator (9 templates: SSP starter, POA&M starter,
  inheritance worksheet, implementation statement worksheet,
  STIG evidence checklist, assessment planning, evidence matrix,
  reciprocity checklist, ConMon calendar) plus 15 short
  plain-language playbooks. It explicitly disclaims being a GRC
  tool, evidence processor, or authorization workflow. Fair
  enough — I judged it as a reference aid and starter kit.
- What slice of the real job it could touch: the lookup slice
  ("what is this control, what baseline is it in, what CCIs hang
  off it") and the blank-page slice (first draft of a tracker or
  worksheet). Maybe 10-15 percent of my week on a bad week.
- What it does not touch at all: everything in eMASS. Package
  assembly, test results, POA&M lifecycle, artifact management,
  inheritance from actual providers, ODP values, tailoring
  decisions, assessment execution, ConMon reporting. That is
  fine — it never claimed to. But inside the slice it does
  claim, the data has to be right, and it is not.

5. STRESS-TEST RESULTS
----------------------------------------------------------------
SCENARIO:    Inheritance (common / hybrid / fully inherited)
RESULT:      PARTIAL
DETAIL:      The concept exists — a Control Inheritance playbook
             (correct warnings: verify customer-side config, do
             not inherit from a provider without an active ATO,
             related controls PL-2 / SA-9) and an Inheritance
             Worksheet template. But control records carry no
             inheritance dimension at all; every control is
             presented as if it were system-specific. For a
             public reference that is survivable. For the
             "worksheet" ambition it means the hybrid
             responsibility split — the part that actually burns
             people — lives entirely in my head, as usual.

SCENARIO:    Tailored-out / N-A control
RESULT:      BROKE
DETAIL:      No concept of tailoring anywhere I could find. The
             baseline is presented as gospel. Worse, the
             Implementation Statement Worksheet does the
             opposite of tailoring: it emits the ENTIRE Rev. 5
             catalog (~1,195 rows including every enhancement,
             in no baseline at all) and asks me to draft a
             statement for each. There is no Low/Mod/High
             selector on the generator. Nobody implements the
             whole catalog. The POA&M template's "Deviation
             Reference" column is the only nod to reality.

SCENARIO:    ODPs (organization-defined parameters)
RESULT:      BROKE — this is the credibility kill shot
DETAIL:      ODPs are not acknowledged to exist. They are not
             shown as blanks, brackets, or prompts — they are
             silently deleted from text labeled "OFFICIAL TEXT /
             SOURCE EXCERPT." AC-2: "Require approvals by for
             requests to create accounts"; "in accordance with ;".
             AC-2(1): "Support the management of system accounts
             using ." A tool that presents parameter-stripped
             text as official teaches juniors wrong and gives
             seniors a reason to close the tab.

SCENARIO:    Enhancement-heavy control (AC-2 and its 13
             enhancements)
RESULT:      BROKE
DETAIL:      Enhancement records exist (as "AC-2.1", dot
             notation) and carry correct baseline placement
             (AC-2(1) = MODERATE/HIGH). But: searching "AC-2"
             returns one control and one assessment procedure —
             no enhancements. Searching "AC-2(1)", the notation
             every practitioner on earth uses, returns 44
             irrelevant DoD Zero Trust activities and zero
             controls. The AC-2 record page itself has no
             "Enhancements" group — connections list CCIs,
             baselines, and APs only. The base-to-enhancement
             relationship, the thing that makes AC-2 navigable,
             is simply not wired into navigation or search.

SCENARIO:    Cross-framework mapping (STIG -> CCI -> control)
RESULT:      BROKE (feature is a facade)
DETAIL:      The "STIG/SRG to controls" comparison — a headline
             use case on the landing page — contains exactly ONE
             STIG item in the entire dataset: "V-100001 Configure
             account management" from a "DISA Public STIG
             Library" benchmark. That is sample/demo data. The
             chain UI itself (official vs inferred links, shared
             vs unmapped CCI counts, mapping trace) is nicely
             designed, but it is a well-built bridge to a town
             with one house. Advertising "STIGs" on the landing
             page with this dataset is exactly the kind of
             overpromise that gets tools demoed to colonels and
             deleted by staff.

SCENARIO:    "And then what" (act on what you found)
RESULT:      PARTIAL
DETAIL:      Better than most reference sites: compare results
             export to CSV/Markdown/JSON, templates generate
             locally with a provenance footer, records have
             stable copyable URLs. But the trail dead-ends where
             it matters: nothing is in a mandated format, the
             worksheet dumps 1,195 unsorted-for-humans rows
             (lexicographic: AC-1, AC-10, AC-11, ... AC-2), CSVs
             open in Excel with "#" comment lines sitting above
             the header row, and "Open official source document"
             on a control record links to the ROOT of the
             usnistgov/oscal-content GitHub repo — not to the
             control, not even to the catalog file. That is not
             a citation, that is a shrug.

6. OUTPUT USABILITY
----------------------------------------------------------------
- Ways to get data out: (1) 9 template generators, each in CSV /
  Markdown / JSON, generated client-side with a source-version
  footer; (2) Export CSV/Markdown/JSON on compare results; (3)
  Copy link on records; (4) no export of a record's own
  connection list that I found from the record page itself.
- Does it survive contact with a real package? Mostly no.
  POA&M Starter: sensible internal-tracker columns (Weakness ID,
  Discovery Source, Related Control, Related STIG/SRG, Risk
  Statement, Severity H/M/L, Milestones, Deviation Reference,
  Risk Acceptance Reference, Evidence Needed for Closure) — but
  it is not the eMASS import format and not the FedRAMP POA&M
  workbook. No CAT I/II/III, no original detection date, no
  resources-required (OMB expects that), no POC fields. Anything
  entered here gets re-keyed into the system of record later —
  the classic two-sources-of-truth trap. Implementation
  Statement Worksheet: full-catalog dump including withdrawn
  controls, no baseline filter — dead on arrival for a real SSP.
  The comment lines above the CSV header will also annoy every
  tool and analyst that touches the file.
- Verdict: PARTIAL. The plumbing for output is genuinely there;
  the artifacts flowing through it are not package-grade.

7. DATA TRUST AUDIT
----------------------------------------------------------------
- Provenance: better presented than most. A Sources registry
  (44 entries, categorized: Federal program 2, Federal published
  29, Federal referenced 6, Mandated 3, MITRE 4) with status
  flags, an official-vs-inferred link taxonomy, and a version
  stamp in export footers ("SP 800-53 Rev. 5 (version
  2026-06-09)"). Rev. 5 is current and correctly identified.
  Credit where due: most tools in this space cannot even tell
  you what revision they ingested.
- Spot-checks performed:
  * AC-2 baseline placement LOW/MODERATE/HIGH/LI-SaaS — correct.
  * AC-2(1) placement MODERATE/HIGH — correct.
  * AC-2(1) title "Automated System Account Management" — correct.
  * CCI-002115 definition "Specify authorized users of the
    system" — consistent with the DISA CCI decomposition of
    AC-2a/d. Plausible; could not verify the full 35-CCI set
    in-tool because list rows show no definitions (UNVERIFIED).
  * AC-2 official text — WRONG AS PRESENTED. All ODP assignments
    silently removed from text labeled official. Same on
    AC-2(1), where the enhancement text ends "using ." and the
    800-53A assessment objects are concatenated into one
    unpunctuated run-on paragraph.
  * Implementation Statement Worksheet rows — WRONG. Includes
    AC-13 (withdrawn), SA-12 (withdrawn in Rev. 5, superseded by
    SR family), SA-13 (withdrawn). The generator ingested the
    OSCAL catalog without filtering withdrawn controls, then
    asks users to write implementation statements for them.
  * STIG dataset — one sample rule (V-100001). Not real DISA
    benchmark content. UNVERIFIED whether any real STIG data
    exists anywhere in the tool; none was findable.
  * Trust labels — every single mapping I expanded carries the
    identical blanket label "High-confidence mapping directly
    supported by the cited source." A trust label that is always
    green is not a trust label, it is wallpaper.
- Trust impact: FOUND ERRORS. Two structural (ODP stripping,
  withdrawn controls in exports), one honesty gap (STIG claim vs
  one demo row). The provenance scaffolding is real and better
  than average; the data flowing through it fails the first
  spot-check a practitioner would run.

8. FINDINGS (detailed)
----------------------------------------------------------------
ID:          CA-VET-001
SURFACE:     Control record pages ("OFFICIAL TEXT / SOURCE
             EXCERPT"), e.g. /record/nist-800-53/AC-2, AC-2.1
AXIS:        DATA
SEVERITY:    DEALBREAKER
WHAT HAPPENED: ODP assignments are silently stripped from control
             text presented as official. "Require for group and
             role membership." "Require approvals by for requests
             to create accounts." "Support the management of
             system accounts using ."
WHY IT MATTERS: ODPs are where compliance actually lives — the
             frequency, the personnel, the time period ARE the
             requirement. Text without them is not a summary, it
             is wrong, and labeling it "official" means a junior
             ISSO will paste it into a document I then have to
             fix in front of an assessor.
SUGGESTION:  Render ODPs as bracketed placeholders straight from
             the OSCAL params — the data is already in the file
             they ingested.

ID:          CA-VET-002
SURFACE:     Templates > Implementation Statement Worksheet
AXIS:        DATA
SEVERITY:    DEALBREAKER
WHAT HAPPENED: The generated worksheet includes withdrawn
             controls — AC-13, SA-12, SA-13 — as rows with
             prompts to describe how they are implemented.
WHY IT MATTERS: Writing an implementation statement for a
             control withdrawn from the catalog is an instant
             credibility loss with any assessor, and the people
             most likely to use a "starter worksheet" are
             exactly the people who will not know to delete
             those rows.
SUGGESTION:  Filter on the OSCAL "withdrawn" status property
             during generation.

ID:          CA-VET-003
SURFACE:     Explore search
AXIS:        FIDELITY
SEVERITY:    SERIOUS
WHAT HAPPENED: Searching "AC-2(1)" — the universal practitioner
             notation — returns 44 DoD Zero Trust activities and
             zero controls. The enhancement exists in the system
             as "AC-2.1" but nothing tells you that. Searching
             "AC-2" does not surface enhancements either.
WHY IT MATTERS: If I cannot find AC-2(1) by typing AC-2(1), the
             tool has failed the most basic query in the domain.
             This alone would end most people's first session.
SUGGESTION:  Normalize paren notation to dot notation in the
             search index; one alias table fixes this.

ID:          CA-VET-004

WHY IT MATTERS: Base-plus-enhancements is THE structural

9. WHAT IT ACTUALLY GETS RIGHT
----------------------------------------------------------------
- The posture: public, no account, no data upload, browser-only
  generation, MIT license, aggressive honesty about not being a
  GRC tool or an authority. That is the correct shape for a
  reference aid and it is respected here, not just claimed.
- The domain map: playbook topics and template selection
  (inheritance worksheet, reciprocity checklist, evidence
  expectation matrix) target the real, unglamorous failure
  points of A&A work.
- Provenance scaffolding: a sources registry with categories and
  status, an official-vs-inferred link taxonomy, version-stamped
  exports, Rev. 5 correctly identified as the catalog.
- Baseline placement data spot-checked correct (AC-2, AC-2(1)).
- Export plumbing exists everywhere (CSV/MD/JSON), which is more
  than most read-only reference sites bother with.
----------------------------------------------------------------
10. WHAT WOULD MAKE ME ACTUALLY USE THIS
----------------------------------------------------------------
1. Fix the text pipeline. Render ODPs as visible bracketed
   parameters and stop labeling stripped text "official." This
   is the single change that moves the tool from "cannot trust"
   to "can cite." Everything else is secondary.
2. Filter withdrawn controls out of every export, and add a
   baseline (Low/Mod/High/FedRAMP) selector plus numeric sort to
   the worksheet generators so the output starts where a real
   SSP starts.
3. Make search speak practitioner: "AC-2(1)" must find AC-2(1),
   and the AC-2 record must list its enhancements.
4. Either ship real STIG content (one real DISA benchmark would
   do) or pull the STIG claim from the landing page until it is
   true.
5. Deep-link provenance per control (NIST catalog page, CCI list
   entry), and put CCI definitions inline in connection lists so
   verification is one glance, not 35 clicks.
   Do those five and this becomes the thing I open to answer
   "what is this control and what hangs off it" and to hand
   PMs a link instead of writing the same explanation email —
   SITUATIONAL, honestly earned. Today it is a well-mannered
   shelf ornament with a data problem.
